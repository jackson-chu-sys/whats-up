☀️ CityShade | White Paper Release: Distributed Deployable PV Shading as Urban Heat-Dome-Adaptive Infrastructure

🔥 The problem
Heat domes are turning into a city-scale, multi-week threat. High-pressure subsidence plus the urban heat island effect creates a positive feedback loop — the more we run air conditioners, the hotter the city gets. Heat events that once lasted 3–5 days now stretch to 10–15.

💡 Our thesis
Don't just block the sun — convert it. Distributed deployable photovoltaic shading intercepts the solar shortwave radiation that would otherwise heat asphalt and buildings, and turns it into electricity. The result: a negative net thermal effect.

📊 By the numbers (engineering estimates)
▶ Local cooling under the shade: ~3–8°C at summer peak (surface temperature down 10–20°C)
▶ Near-surface heat load cut from ~85% (unshaded) to ~20% — roughly three-quarters
▶ District-average cooling scales with coverage: ~0.05–0.1°C at 10% → ~0.3–0.5°C at 50%
▶ Parking-lot scenario: ~3.8-year payback, 18–22% IRR
▶ Single system: 4.7 kWh/day in summer, only 0.3 kWh self-consumption

🏗️ Five deployment scenarios
▶ Parking lots — the most mature, scalably replicable direction ("PV + storage + charging" loop)
▶ Leisure spaces — umbrella-petal canopies for plazas and parks
▶ Slow-traffic roads — zigzag folding grilles forming continuous shade corridors
▶ Fast-traffic roads — tubular-truss gantries cooling expressway pavement
▶ Building facades — single-roller flexible PV curtains balancing shade and daylight

🌏 Why now
China's PV supply chain — over 80% of global capacity from polysilicon to modules — gives this route a structural cost and resilience advantage. Urban cooling demand meets PV capacity absorption: a two-way positive feedback.

🤝 Our position
This is not geoengineering. It is reversible, measurable, free of cross-boundary spillover, and economically positive — a genuine "no-regret" intervention. Parking lots lead scaled rollout; deployability is an optional enhancement for extreme weather, not the core pitch. Standards, approval, supply chain, and O&M remain the key challenges to scale.

We welcome discussion with researchers, urban planners, and industry partners on pilots and standards.
