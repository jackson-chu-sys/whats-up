## ✍️ "Morally Binding": Six AI Giants Sign a Penalty-Free Superintelligence Compact with Trump

After a White House lunch on September 29, Trump announced a "Superintelligence Compact: Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibility" with Anthropic (Amodei), Google (Pichai), Meta (Zuckerberg), OpenAI (president Brockman), Nvidia (Huang) and xAI (Musk): four voluntary measures — robust internal controls, independent external audits, board-level committees, and a roughly ten-person group to "oversee the whole field." Trump repeatedly stressed the compact is "morally binding," with no penalties, no compliance deadlines and no enforcement mechanism — while just two weeks earlier, 22 heads of state had signed a UN General Assembly call for global AI guardrails and the US rejected any international binding attempt. The contrast with Europe is stark: AI Act enforcement over general-purpose models took effect on August 2, letting the AI Office demand documentation, evaluate models, restrict distribution and issue fines, with transparency duties and complaint and whistleblower channels all live. America's "self-police to avoid being policed" versus Europe's "rules first": global AI governance has formally split into two tracks — and Zuckerberg's and Huang's public backing of self-regulation, downplaying their peers' calls for oversight, is the footnote.

## 🛰️ Google's TPU Goes to Space: SpaceX Completes the First Hardware Validation of an Orbital Data Center

On October 1, a Falcon 9 lifted off from Vandenberg carrying a Planet Labs solar-powered prototype satellite with four Google TPUs aboard — moving Project Suncatcher from slideware into hardware testing: validating radiation tolerance, thermal management and on-orbit compute, with two more test satellites and an inter-satellite laser link next year and a target of one year of orbital operation. It is the key leap in the AI-infrastructure narrative: ground data centers are hitting hard walls of power, land, permitting and NIMBY opposition (SpaceX's term for orbit is "unlimited real estate"), while orbital solar is theoretically abundant and cooling is radiative. The skeptic list is just as long: no repairs, no upgrades, radiation degradation, orbital debris, bandwidth and latency. But the real question was never whether space compute pencils out today — it is how long the ground's electricity prices and permitting ceiling can hold as launch costs keep falling. The majors are voting with their money: the same week, Hacker News's front page still carried an analysis of "cracks in the data-center boom," while SpaceX, Google and Blue Origin keep hauling compute skyward. The energy arms race has officially moved from the grid to orbit.

## ⚡ A Historic Crossover: the IEA Says Renewables Will Out-Generate Coal This Year

The IEA's Electricity Mid-Year Update 2026, published this week, lands a milestone judgment: in 2026 renewable generation formally overtakes coal worldwide (2025 was already near parity), with its share rising from 33% toward 37% by 2027. Solar is the biggest engine of growth — another roughly 600 TWh this year (+23%) — and in 2026 it passes wind to become the world's second-largest renewable source after hydro. Demand is accelerating in step: global electricity demand grows 3.6% this year and 3.8% next, reaching 30,700 TWh in 2027, with AI data centers and electrification jointly lifting the curve. But the same report pours cold water: the Hormuz LNG disruption has pushed Asian and European gas to its highest since the 2022–23 crisis, several countries are switching back from gas to coal, and power-sector CO2 rises about 1% this year before plateauing in 2027. The direction is right — carbon intensity falls from 435 to about 410 g/kWh by 2027, and EU power emissions may drop below half their 2017 level next year — but the historic crossover arrives amid a fossil-fuel bounce. The transition is not a straight line; it is a curve with geopolitical potholes.
