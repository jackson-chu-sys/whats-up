## ▶ The Hottest October on Record: El Niño's Niño 3.4 Anomaly at 3.21°C Breaks Every Mark

Copernicus confirmed on November 6 that October 2026 averaged 15.35°C globally — 0.90°C above the 1991–2020 mean and 1.75°C above pre-industrial levels, the third consecutive month above 1.5°C. Sea-surface anomalies in the equatorial Pacific's Niño 3.4 region reached 3.21°C, above the peaks of the 1982, 1997 and 2015 super El Niños, while global sea-surface temperatures outside the polar regions hit an October record of 20.95°C. 2026 is now almost certain to become only the second calendar year after 2024 to run above 1.5°C. Kara Sea ice hit an October low.

## ▶ COP31 Opens in Antalya Tomorrow: Two Hosts, Two Presidents, and the Theme Is Delivery

COP31 runs November 9–20 in Antalya with delegations from nearly 190 countries. In an unusual arrangement, Turkey hosts while Australia runs the negotiations — Chris Bowen as negotiating president, Turkey's environment minister Murat Kurum as conference president — the compromise that broke last year's bidding deadlock. The theme is "a COP of the future, a COP of delivery," built on three pillars of dialogue, consensus and action, with one goal of raising electricity's share of global energy consumption from roughly 20% to 35% by 2035. Bowen says the outcome has to be something "people can see is real."

## ▶ A New Gas Field Off Egypt: About 200 Billion Cubic Feet, 22 Kilometres From Existing Kit

Egypt's petroleum ministry said on November 6 that the Nessim-1X exploration well in the Temsah block has hit gas, with preliminary reserves estimated at about 200 billion cubic feet across roughly 15 metres of net pay. The operator is a joint venture of Italy's Eni and Australia's Woodside, with drilling carried out by Petrobel, Eni's venture with state oil company EGPC. To save time, a roughly 22-kilometre pipeline will tie the find into the Abu Madi–El Gamil trunk line, using existing facilities to shorten the path to first production. Egypt has spent two years pushing domestic output to displace imports, and a better investment climate plus cleared arrears are the preconditions for this exploration revival.

## ▶ KCL-286: a Drug That Cleared Phase I Safety Starts Repairing Alzheimer's DNA Breaks

A King's College London team reports in FEBS Open Bio that KCL-286, a small molecule originally developed for spinal-cord injury, activates retinoic acid receptor beta, upregulates BRCA1, repairs neuronal DNA double-strand breaks and suppresses the inflammatory activation of microglia and astrocytes. In 18-month-old Tg2576 Alzheimer's mice, γH2AX signalling in the brain fell significantly. The advantage is that the compound has already completed Phase I human safety and tolerability trials and crosses the blood-brain barrier orally; if the mechanism holds in people it could save years of development. For now the data are still from mice.
