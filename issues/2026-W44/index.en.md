## ▶ DeepSeek Proposes a "Super-agent" Architecture: Agents That Stop Living in One Chat Window

Late on October 31, DeepSeek's Harness team published "Explorations in Super-agent Design." The core judgement is blunt: a person can track only 3-5 active conversations at once — the multitasking ceiling is human attention, not the model. The answer is a two-layer architecture with event-driven, asynchronous multi-channel messaging, separated storage and compute, and an external watchdog, so the system owns conversation management and context sync. An internal instance already runs: when someone reports a bug in a group chat without @-ing the AI, it reproduces the issue in the background and hunts for a fix. The team stresses this is engineering exploration, not a product launch.

🔗 Sources: [Jiemian: super-agent design](https://m.jiemian.com/article/15278252.html) · [SCTV: the technical post](https://www.sctv.com/news/2116552553092603906)

## ▶ MIT Builds a Programmable Nanoparticle Cancer Vaccine: Antigen Density, Now a Dial

An MIT team turned harmless M13 bacteriophage into nanofibers coated with cancer antigens and — by adding one or two negatively charged amino acids plus a mutation that slows antigen-protein synthesis — tuned antigen density precisely from 6% to 70%. In mice, 35% density gave the strongest protection: dendritic cells took it up more readily, T-cell responses were stronger, and later challenges with melanoma and with colon tumours expressing an ADPGK neoantigen barely formed tumours. The phage's own single-stranded DNA acts as a built-in adjuvant through TLR9, so no extra formulation is needed. A key parameter of personalized vaccines has become, for the first time, a designable knob.

🔗 Sources: [MIT News: programmable nanovaccine](https://news.mit.edu/2026/mit-engineers-create-nanoparticles-delivering-personalized-cancer-vaccines-1030) · [Phys.org: mouse data](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-customizable-nanoparticles-potential-personalized-cancer.html)

## ▶ A New Obesity Target, SPHK2: Skip GLP-1 and Make Fat Burn Itself

A VCU Massey team reports in Science Advances that deleting sphingosine kinase 2 (SPHK2) specifically in fat cells made mice resist high-fat-diet obesity: energy expenditure rose, subcutaneous fat turned beige, thermogenic genes such as Ucp1 were upregulated, glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity improved and liver fat fell. The mechanism: an obesogenic diet drives SPHK2 into the nucleus, where it partners with transcription factors to suppress the thermogenic programme. With GLP-1 drugs failing some patients and weight rebounding after stopping, this "let fat burn itself" route offers another path — today's inhibitors still have off-target effects, and more specific molecules are on the way.

🔗 Sources: [MedicalXpress: the fat-cell enzyme](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-fat-cell-enzyme-potential-strategy.html) · [Science Advances: the paper](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aec7066)
